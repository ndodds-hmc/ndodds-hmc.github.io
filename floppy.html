💾 Deep Dive: The 3.5" Floppy Disk

The King of 90s Storage

Before cloud storage, USB thumb drives, or even rewriteable CDs, the 3.5-inch High-Density Floppy Disk was how data moved from computer to computer. Introduced by Sony in the 1980s, it reached its peak cultural dominance in the mid-to-late 1990s.

Ironically, despite being called "floppy," the 3.5-inch variety was housed in a rigid plastic shell. The name was a leftover legacy from its predecessor, the truly flexible 5.25-inch and 8-inch disks.

Technical Specifications

To put things into perspective for the modern user, here is what a premium, top-of-the-line 3.5" disk offered in 1995:

Specification Standard Value
Formatted Capacity 1.44 MB (Megabytes)
Physical Size 90mm x 94mm x 3.3mm
Write-Protection Physical sliding tab in the corner
Average File Capacity ~1 text document or 1 low-res JPEG image
⚠️ VINTAGE TECH TIP: Keep your floppy disks away from magnets, speakers, and CRT monitors! One accidental swipe near a magnetic field could corrupt your entire school project.
